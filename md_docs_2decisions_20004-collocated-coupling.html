0004 — Collocated state and pressure–velocity coupling

  • Status: accepted
  • Date: 2026-10-30
  • Owner: saturnCFD v1
  • Supersedes: none
  • Protocol: docs/protocols/finiteVolume.md
  • Tests: tests/fv/..., tests/solvers/...

Context

The mesh is structured hexahedral, but the solver must still choose where the velocity and pressure unknowns live. A staggered MAC grid gives exact discrete continuity and no checkerboard mode, but splits the velocity components onto different face meshes, complicates the vector form and complicates the .msh and 2D/3D handling. A collocated cell-centred grid puts all unknowns at cell centroids, makes the vector form natural and matches OpenFOAM, Basilisk's default centred solver and the collocated mixed spaces of Nektar++/NekRS.

Decision

Use a collocated, cell-centred finite-volume formulation.

  • Velocity \(\mathbf{u}_P=(u_P,v_P,w_P)\) and dynamic pressure \(p_P\) live at the cell centroid.
  • Convective and diffusive fluxes are evaluated on faces; face values are interpolated from the two adjacent cells.
  • Suppress the checkerboard pressure mode with Rhie–Chow momentum interpolation on the face flux.
  • SIMPLE for steady Stokes and steady incompressible flow.
  • PISO for transient incompressible flow.
  • The vector momentum equation is assembled and solved as a vector system: one operator, three components (block size 3 or multiple right-hand sides).
  • A staggered MAC backend is not implemented in v1.0. It may be added later as a specialised structured-grid option.

Alternatives considered

alternative why not chosen
Staggered MAC exact continuity and no checkerboard, but splits components, complicates vector form and .msh/2D handling
Collocated without Rhie–Chow checkerboard pressure; the discrete gradient/divergence stencil decouples odd/even cells
Collocated with BCG projection Basilisk-style and accurate, but harder to combine with assembled vector implicit solves and PETSc
Mixed Taylor–Hood finite elements stable, but changes the discretisation from finite volume and the .msh cell logic

Consequences

  • Code: CellField<Real> for pressure and scalars, VectorCellField for velocity, FaceField for fluxes; a Rhie–Chow face-flux routine.
  • Linear algebra: vector momentum matrix (block 3) and scalar pressure matrix; good fit for PETSc.
  • Accuracy: Rhie–Chow introduces a pressure-gradient correction; it must be derived and documented, not treated as a magic interpolation.
  • Risk: checkerboard can reappear at boundaries or after a bad under-relaxation choice; the divergence norm and pressure field are mandatory diagnostics.
  • Future: a MAC option can be added behind the same solver protocol if a structured AMR path is introduced.

Validation / exit criteria

  • Plane Poiseuille and Couette profiles match the analytic solution.
  • Lid-driven cavity at \(Re\to0\) converges and has no checkerboard pressure.
  • The discrete divergence norm is below a stated tolerance after each pressure correction.
  • The pressure null space is handled explicitly and the mean pressure is reported.
