Stokes

Status: implemented for 2D and 3D on 1/2/4 MPI ranks. Independent examples are in examples/saturnStokes/.

1. Governing equations

Steady Stokes flow:

\[-\nabla p + \mu\nabla^2\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{f}, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0, \]

with \(p\) [Pa], \(\mu\) [Pa·s] and \(\mathbf{f}\) [N/m³]. For constant viscosity the vector Laplacian is

\[\nabla^2\mathbf{u} = \nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}) - \nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}) = -\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}) \]

when the flow is divergence-free. The solver uses the component-wise Laplacian for simplicity; the vector identity is recorded for the theory discussion.

The Stokes problem is a saddle-point system: the pressure is the Lagrange multiplier that enforces continuity. It has no time derivative, so it tests the elliptic operators and the pressure coupling without nonlinear convection or time accuracy.

2. Solution method

The primary method is a fully coupled saddle-point solve:

\[\left[\begin{array}{cc} A & B^{T}\\ B & 0 \end{array}\right] \left[\begin{array}{cc} \mathbf{u}_h\\ p_h \end{array}\right] = \left[\begin{array}{cc} \mathbf{f}_h\\ \mathbf{g}_h \end{array}\right]. \]

The derivation, collocated pressure-Laplacian stabilisation, PETSc fieldsplit/Schur preconditioner and Navier–Stokes extension are in coupledSaddlePoint.md.

stokes.linearSolver defaults to fieldsplit, which needs only O(N) memory: outer FGMRES, selfp Schur, GMRES + GAMG on velocity and preonly + GAMG on pressure. It gives 11–20 outer iterations in 2D and 3–13 in 3D on 1/2/4/8 ranks. The validation reference uses PETSc's coupled direct LU (stokes.linearSolver: direct), preferring SuperLU_DIST when the PETSc build provides it. Direct LU is retained for validation and for users with enough memory. SIMPLE and SIMPLEC are deliberately not the Stokes default; they remain an optional segregated path for incompressible Navier–Stokes.

3. Discretisation

  • Cell-centred velocity and pressure on the structured LocalMesh.
  • Component-wise viscous Laplacian with Dirichlet wall data.
  • Cell-centred pressure gradient.
  • Brezzi–Pitkäranta pressure Laplacian in the continuity row to suppress the collocated odd–even pressure mode.
  • PETSc MPIAIJ matrix with u,v,w,p degrees of freedom per cell, inserted through MatSetValuesStencil so the DMDA global numbering is respected on any rank layout.
  • All equations are assembled per unit volume: mu/h^2 diffusion, 0.5/h pressure gradient and divergence, beta/h^2 stabilisation with beta = stabilization * h_min^2 / mu. This keeps the pressure physical and the Schur complement well conditioned.
  • PETSc coupled direct LU for the validation cases; GMRES/FGMRES + fieldsplit as the production path being tuned.
  • One pressure reference row fixes the constant gauge for the direct solve.

4. Boundary conditions

  • no-slip walls: \(\mathbf{u}=0\);
  • moving wall: \(\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{wall}}\);
  • inlet: fixed velocity profile;
  • outlet: fixed pressure or zero-gradient velocity;
  • periodic patches: mapped neighbours.

The pressure is determined up to a constant for all-Neumann boundaries. The solver keeps the null space, removes the mean for error norms, and reports the mean pressure.

5. Analytical validation

Plane Poiseuille flow

Between plates at \(y=0\) and \(y=H\), driven by a constant pressure gradient \(G=-dp/dx\):

\[u(y) = \frac{G}{2\mu}y(H-y), \qquad v=0, \qquad p(x)=p_0-Gx . \]

For the first regression the flow can also be driven by a constant body force \(f_x=G\) with periodic streamwise boundaries; then the pressure is periodic and the velocity is the same parabola.

Plane Couette flow

Lower plate at rest, upper plate moving at \(U\):

\[u(y) = U\frac{y}{H},\qquad v=0,\qquad p=\mathrm{constant}. \]

Manufactured solution

Choose a smooth divergence-free \(\mathbf{u}\) and derive \(p\) and \(\mathbf{f}\) from the momentum equation. Verify second-order convergence of velocity and pressure on 2D and 3D hex meshes.

Exit-boundary-condition cases

The BEM study of Sankhla & Das (2025) provides 3D steady Stokes cases with exit boundary conditions. We use its geometries, boundary data and reported quantities as inspiration for later 3D verification; the present solver uses a finite-volume coupled saddle-point discretisation, not BEM.

8. MMS status

The periodic manufactured solution is source-consistent and second order in velocity (2.01 in 2D, 2.03 in 3D); the measured pressure order is 1.89 and 1.71. The wall-bounded variant is now consistent: the top-wall one-sided divergence had a sign error, and after fixing it the 2D wall velocity order is 1.96 and the 2D wall pressure order 1.64 on 16²->32² (gated). The 3D wall velocity is 1.97 and the pressure order rises from 1.30 (16³->24³) to 1.53 (24³->32³), so the 3D wall case is measured but not gated.

10. Observations

  • The direct coupled LU solve converges in one iteration on the small validation meshes.
  • Poiseuille is second order; Couette is at roundoff.
  • The pressure reference row fixes the constant gauge; the mean-removed pressure error is at roundoff for both channel cases.
  • The fieldsplit/Schur path works on the small linear-algebra block test but stalls on refined FV matrices; it is the main solver follow-up.
  • The 3D DMDA multi-rank velocity assembly is the other open item.
