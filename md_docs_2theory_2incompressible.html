Incompressible Navier–Stokes

Status: fourth solver in the v1 sequence (ADR 0001, 0004).

1. Governing equations

\[\rho\left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}) \right) = -\nabla p + \mu\nabla^2\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{f}, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0, \]

or, divided by \(\rho\),

\[\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}) = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu\nabla^2\mathbf{u} + \frac{\mathbf{f}}{\rho}, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0. \]

The pressure is dynamic pressure [Pa]. For constant density, the kinematic pressure \(p/\rho\) is an alternative; v1 stores dynamic pressure for the two-phase future.

The Reynolds number is

\[Re = \frac{\rho U L}{\mu}. \]

2. Discretisation

Cell-centred finite volume on the structured hexahedral mesh:

\[V_P\frac{d(\rho\mathbf{u}_P)}{dt} + \sum_f \dot m_f \mathbf{u}_f = -\sum_f p_f\mathbf{S}_f + \sum_f \mu\nabla\mathbf{u}_f\cdot\mathbf{S}_f + V_P\mathbf{f}_P. \]

The convective face value uses upwind or a TVD/MUSCL reconstruction; the diffusive term uses the non-orthogonal correction. The face flux is Rhie–Chow-corrected to suppress checkerboarding.

The discrete continuity equation is

\[\frac{1}{V_P}\sum_f \mathbf{u}_f\cdot\mathbf{S}_f = 0. \]

3. Time integration: PISO

  1. Momentum predictor with the old pressure \(p^n\):

    \[ a_P\mathbf{u}_P^* = \sum_N a_N\mathbf{u}_N^* + \mathbf{b}_P - V_P(\nabla p^n)_P. \]

  2. Compute face fluxes with Rhie–Chow.
  3. Pressure correction \(p'\):

    \[ \nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\nabla p'\right) = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}^*. \]

  4. Velocity correction:

    \[ \mathbf{u}^{**} = \mathbf{u}^* - \frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\nabla p', \qquad p^* = p^n + \alpha_p p'. \]

  5. Recompute the face flux and apply a second pressure correction (PISO corrector). Two correctors are the default; the number is a YAML option.
  6. Advance to \(n+1\).

Backward Euler is the first time integrator; Crank–Nicolson is a later option. The convection can be explicit or semi-implicit; diffusion is implicit and solved with PETSc. The vector momentum system uses block size 3 or three right-hand sides.

A Chorin projection is the one-corrector limit of PISO; it is useful for debugging but not the production path.

4. Boundary conditions

  • no-slip and moving walls;
  • inlet velocity profile;
  • outlet pressure or zero-gradient velocity;
  • symmetry;
  • periodic patches;
  • empty for the inactive direction of a 2D mesh.

Every boundary face has exactly one BC. The pressure-correction equation uses homogeneous Neumann on walls and a fixed value or zero-gradient on open boundaries.

5. Validation

Lid-driven cavity

Unit square, top lid at \(U=1\), other walls no-slip. Compare the vertical centreline \(u(y)\) and horizontal centreline \(v(x)\) against Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982) at \(Re=100\), then \(Re=1000\). Report RMS and maximum deviation, the minimum location and the zero-crossing.

The v0 implementation reached RMS 0.0024 at \(Re=100\), 128², with a staggered projection. v1 must reproduce or improve that on the collocated PISO path.

Taylor–Green vortex

Periodic 2D/3D decaying vortex. Check the kinetic-energy decay rate and the convergence under refinement. It is a clean test of convection, diffusion and pressure coupling without walls.

Channel flow

Plane channel at a target friction Reynolds number (later); first validate the laminar parabolic profile.

6. Diagnostics

Every step or output interval:

  • maximum velocity and kinetic energy;
  • \(\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_2\) and \(\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_\infty\);
  • pressure mean and range;
  • momentum residual, pressure-correction residual, PETSc iterations;
  • CFL and active time-step limit.

7. Observations (to be filled from runs)

Record:

  • the divergence norm with and without Rhie–Chow;
  • the PISO corrector count needed for a stable solution;
  • the effect of the limiter on the cavity centreline;
  • the cost of the pressure solve relative to one time step;
  • the difference between backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson;
  • any checkerboard or pressure drift at the lid corner.
